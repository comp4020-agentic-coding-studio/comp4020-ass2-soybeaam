# Cognitive Dissonance and Self-Justification

Week 3 — the excuses we believe are the ones that last

---

## The most durable excuse isn't the cleverest one

It's the one the speaker has already talked themselves into.

Today: why that happens, and why it makes an excuse harder to argue with.

---

## Festinger's prediction

Holding two contradictory beliefs is uncomfortable.

When behaviour and self-image conflict, the mind doesn't always
correct the behaviour — it often edits the belief instead.

---

## A lie vs. a rationalisation

- A lie: the speaker knows the excuse is false
- A rationalisation: the speaker has revised their own account until it feels true

Same words can come from either state. The difference is invisible
from the outside — and it changes what kind of argument will work.

# You cannot fact-check your way out of a belief someone needed to hold.

---

## Why self-justification resists dismantling

Pointing out the contradiction doesn't reliably work — dissonance
theory predicts it can entrench the belief further, not loosen it.

The excuse isn't a claim under evaluation anymore. It's load-bearing.
