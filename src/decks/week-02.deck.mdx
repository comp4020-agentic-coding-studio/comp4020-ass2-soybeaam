# The Grammar of Deflection

Week 2 — how an excuse works at the level of the sentence

---

## Last week: what. This week: how.

Week 1 gave you the taxonomy — denial, justification, diffusion.

Today we go down to the grammar that carries it.

---

## "Mistakes were made"

The passive voice deletes the agent grammatically, not just rhetorically.

Compare:

- "I broke the vase." — agent present
- "The vase was broken." — agent optional
- "Mistakes were made." — agent has vanished entirely

---

## Hedges buy time, not distance

"Look." "To be fair." "In fairness."

These don't remove an agent — they signal that a concession is coming
and ask the listener to soften their judgement before it arrives.

---

## Nominalisation hides the doer inside the deed

Turning a verb into a noun strips the subject that used to do it:

- "We decided to lay off staff" → "a decision was made"
- "I failed to file the report" → "there was a failure to file"

The action survives. The actor doesn't.
