# Why We Excuse

Week 1: The Art of the Excuse

---

## An apology accepts a fact

"I was late. I'm sorry."

The fact stands. Nothing about it is renegotiated.

---

## An excuse renegotiates it

"I was late. The trains were down again."

Same fact, but now it's not really about you.

---

# Excuses do work. Apologies don't have to.

---

## A three-part taxonomy

1. **Denial**: the fact didn't happen the way you think
2. **Justification**: the fact happened, but it was the right call
3. **Diffusion of responsibility**: the fact happened, but not because of me

---

## Denial

"I never said that."

Attacks the premise. Cheapest to make, easiest to disprove.

---

## Justification

"I did say that, but you'd have said it too."

Accepts the premise, reframes the standard it's judged against.

---

## Diffusion of responsibility

"I did say that, but my manager told me to."

Accepts the premise, moves the agent.

---

## Where this breaks

Real excuses mix all three in one sentence. The taxonomy is a starting
vocabulary, not a filing system, and you'll spend the rest of the semester
finding its edge cases.
