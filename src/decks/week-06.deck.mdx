# Historical Alibis

Week 6: excuses at the scale of treaties, successions, and wars

---

## Not every excuse stays small

Most excuses in this course have been personal. Some reroute
history instead.

Three documented cases today.

---

## Case one

An excuse offered at the right moment, to the right audience,
changed what happened next, not because it was true, but because
believing it served the audience too.

---

## Case two

A different mechanism: the excuse didn't need to be believed, only
accepted publicly enough that reversing course became too costly.

---

## Case three

A third pattern: the excuse arrived after the fact, and rewrote how
the event was remembered rather than how it was handled at the time.

---

# An excuse lands at scale when the audience has its own reason to believe it.

---

## What separates scale from failure

The taxonomy from week 1 still applies. These are still denial,
justification, or diffusion. What's different is audience incentive:
the people receiving the excuse often benefit from accepting it.
