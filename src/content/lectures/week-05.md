A genuine apology, for this course's purposes, names a fact, accepts an
agent, and states a consequence. Almost no corporate statement clears all
three, and the gap is where the analysis lives.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture, you will be able to:

- state the three-part test for a genuine apology: named fact, accepted agent, stated consequence
- score a real corporate statement against the test and identify which part it fails
- explain why "mistakes were made" and "we regret the impact" fail on different parts of the test

## Outline

- the three-part test: named fact, accepted agent, stated consequence
- "mistakes were made" fails on agent; "we regret the impact" fails on fact
- case studies: three real statements scored against the test, live
- the model the test is cut down from: Benoit's image repair theory, linked below
