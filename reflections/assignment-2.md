# What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was a change in process. My early instinct was to ask to design pages one at a time. That approach, however, would have been slow given how the template was structured. What moved the work forward was taking baby steps in a deliberate order. Before building any page, I filled in the meta context, that is, what the course is, who the audience is, how the site is organised, and how the pages would relate to each other. Only then did I turn to the elements that multiple pages had in common, such as [navigation, layout, styling, headers and footers]. Once those shared foundations were settled, each new page became a much smaller task, or so I thought. The AI struggled to recognise prompts and output a "nice" design, and there were several issues when re-prompting a difference design and still accommodating to phone users. Issues arose especially with side navigation bar, as what I assumed to be a simple task turned into an iterative process and constant re-prompting. During the re-prompting and several processes of Claude explaining the issue was resolved when it wasn't revealed that a higher model, such as opus and screenshots made the AI critically review the output. 

The lesson was that the quality of the output depended on the quality of the context I gave, and that solving a shared problem once is better than solving it twenty times. Progress sped up as soon as I realised this.

# What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It made me want to be a developer who thinks in systems before details. Working this way showed me that the most valuable skill wasn't producing code quickly, but knowing what to decide first: what the structure is, what's shared, what can vary and how to fix it agenticly. I want to keep that habit of designing the foundation first, whether I'm building alone or with AI.

It also changed how I see my role when working with AI tools. I don't want to be someone who passively accepts whatever gets generated. I want to be the person who sets direction, provides clear context, breaks big problems into manageable steps, and checks that the parts fit together. The AI could generate anything, but the elements of the whole site came from my decisions about order and structure.

Going forward, I want to approach projects with that same patience: start small, establish the shared context first, and build up from there.
